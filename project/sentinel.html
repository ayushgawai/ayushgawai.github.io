Sentinel AI: cheap on every frame, VLM only on escalation

Technical deep dive

Ownership

Team project, winner of the HP × SJSU Edge AI Hackathon. I owned the video and frame-streaming backend, asynchronous event routing, alert triggers and incident logging.

Architecture

  • YOLO26s-pose person detection with temporal tracking and per-track motion rules (weapon, fall, run, loitering, sudden change); optional OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 anomaly screening.
  • Score-based escalation instead of a VLM call per frame: a 16-frame event window goes to locally served Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B FP8 on the HP ZGX Nano (GB10) through HP Z Runtime, classifying WEAPON, FIGHT, THEFT, RUN, MEDICAL or BENIGN.
  • Python severity thresholds and an incident state machine: NEW → ALERTED → DISPATCH_PENDING → DISPATCHED → TRACKING → RESOLVED / DISMISSED. SQLite event history; REST, WebSocket and MJPEG console updates.
  • Guarded dispatch path: local ASR + TTS + telephony, human confirmation, kill switch, one dispatch per incident, fixed camera-to-address map.

Demo observations (not benchmarks)

  • The six-feed wall (three with full analysis) ran about 13–14 fps, up from ~3. Demo calls never reached the real 911 service.

Later variant

Meta SAM 3.1 + Muse: 8-second per-camera ring buffers, ByteTrack IDs through weak detections, SAM prompt-based segmentation on enlarged person crops, 8 evidence frames + crop to Muse Spark on Baseten, a text-only verifier, and deterministic guardrails deciding automatic versus operator-approved dispatch.

the design calls

Cost-fidelity funnel

Cheap per-frame perception handles volume; the VLM handles the few ambiguous, high-impact events.

No cloud, on purpose

Video stays on the ZGX Nano, a privacy and latency choice, not a limitation.
