AssistAI — wheelchair obstacle alert

An AI obstacle-alert system for power wheelchairs where the real contribution is the human-machine interface: depth-triggered, multimodal warnings — twelve tactors plus speech or tone cues — measured by how fast a real person responds.

the numbers

24participants × 24 controlled trials

12vibrotactile tactors (seatback + wrist)

0.90confidence gate on YOLOv10n pedestrian detection

3 × 2seatback tactor array in the published setup

how it works

  • Perception: an onboard GPU laptop runs synchronized ZED 2i RGB and depth streams at 30 fps through pretrained YOLOv10n; box-center image-midline sets left/right, and a lightweight temporal association keeps one obstacle stable across frames to avoid duplicate warnings.
  • Trigger: box-center depth drives an inward crossing of roughly 1 m as the warning trigger; a pilot-measured ~1.65 s haptic actuation lag is compensated by movement speed × delay.
  • Warning design: instructional versus hazard-location content, speech versus non-speech cues, and seatback / wrist / no-vibration conditions — 12 warning designs in the expanded study.
  • Findings: seatback vibration was associated with stronger usefulness/satisfaction and, in the expanded study, shorter reaction times than audio-only; action-oriented warnings produced faster reactions and higher usefulness than informative/location-only; non-speech cues improved reaction time when tactile feedback was absent.

the design calls

Measure the human, not just the model

Reaction time and preference are the real metrics of a useful warning.

Trust is engineered

A correct detection is not enough — the cue has to carry the decision to the driver fast.
