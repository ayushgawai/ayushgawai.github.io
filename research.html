Peer-reviewed research paper · 2026

Visual attention from eye-tracking signals

Analysis pipeline over Pupil Labs Core streams: per-trial timestamp alignment, smoothing and invalid-sample rejection, then fixation, blink, pupil-diameter and duration features compared across three attention tasks. In a 7-participant study, fixation count separated the tasks better than any other measured oculometric variable.

Read the paper Conference presentation
  • 7 participants, ages 19–36
  • 3 attention tasks
  • fixation count strongest discriminator
  • Pupil Labs Core gaze, pupil, blink
Technical deep dive

Tasks

  • Global: describe one image. Selective: find differences between two images shown side by side (concurrent). Sustained: compare images shown one after another (sequential).

Signals and pipeline

  • Pupil Labs Core streams: gaze position, fixations, blinks, pupil diameter, with per-trial timestamps.
  • High-frequency signal data, not tabular rows: alignment to trial boundaries, smoothing, invalid-sample handling, then per-trial features (fixation count and percentage, blink count and percentage, pupil diameter, task duration).
  • Gaze heatmaps and attention trajectories for interpretation; modular, documented, re-runnable workflow with model-ready outputs.

Analysis

  • Statistical tests across the global, selective and sustained conditions to find which oculometric features separate tasks.
  • Fixation count was the strongest discriminator among the measured variables, under controlled low-demand conditions.

Scope

  • Small preliminary study, not a powered one. The paper reports eye-tracking measures only; fNIRS belongs to the broader Body Brain Connect track and is not part of these findings.

Co-authored team study. I built the alignment, feature and statistical-comparison pipeline; helped collect and interpret the data.

Two peer-reviewed research papers · 2026

AI obstacle alerts for power wheelchairs

An onboard GPU laptop runs YOLOv10n on synchronized ZED 2i RGB + depth at 30 fps; temporal association keeps one stable track, and an inward box-center depth crossing at ~1 m fires the warning. I owned the perception-to-HMI layer: cue selection, a 3×2 seatback tactor array, lag-compensated timing and timestamped response capture.

Read the journal paper Read the conference paper Conference presentation
  • 24 × 24 participants × trials
  • 12 warning designs
  • ~1.65 s haptic lag, compensated
  • seatback vibration + instructional cue: faster reactions
Technical deep dive

Perception

  • Onboard GPU laptop; synchronized ZED 2i RGB and aligned depth at 30 fps; pretrained YOLOv10n pedestrian detection at a 0.90 confidence threshold.
  • Box center vs image midline sets left/right; lightweight temporal association holds one obstacle across frames so a person does not trigger duplicate warnings.
  • Box-center depth drives the event: an inward crossing of roughly 1 m.

Timing

  • Pilot timing showed ~1.65 s of haptic actuation lag relative to audio. The haptic trigger distance is pushed out by movement speed × actuation delay, so vibration and speech land together at the boundary.
  • Loop: capture → detect/track → boundary crossing → cue and timing selection → actuation → timestamped participant reaction → statistical comparison.

Study

  • 24 able-bodied participants × 24 controlled trials; 12 warning designs crossing vibration configuration (seatback, wrist, none), auditory signal (speech, non-speech) and information type (instructional vs hazard location). The HFES paper is the 6-participant pilot of the same system.
  • Seatback vibration gave shorter reaction times than audio-only and higher usefulness; instructional messages beat informative ones on reaction time and usefulness. Non-speech cues helped when there was no vibration; decision accuracy did not differ across conditions.
  • Measures response timing, usefulness and preference, not real-world crash reduction. Separate from the C++17 camera + LiDAR prototype in the Robots chapter.

Co-authored team publication; the journal paper credits me with Software, Methodology and Investigation. I owned the perception-to-HMI integration, warning timing and cue logic, response capture and the comparative analysis.

SJSU · SO-101 · Hugging Face LeRobot

SO-101 imitation learning: ACT vs SmolVLA

Leader–follower teleoperation on six-joint SO-101 arms (Feetech STS3215 servos on USB motor buses) logs calibrated joint commands with synchronized dual-RGB frames into LeRobot datasets. An ACT policy (ResNet-18 backbone, trained from scratch) and a fine-tuned SmolVLA were trained on the same 50 demos and scored in physical pick-and-place rollouts.

Code on GitHub
  • 46/50 ACT rollouts, fixed cube position
  • ~18 ms ACT local inference (SmolVLA ~251 ms)
  • ~110k frames from 50 demos
  • Lock=1 EEPROM patch unblocked calibration

  1. symptom follower motor IDs reverted after every power cycle; wrist-roll and gripper collided on one ID
  2. cause EEPROM write never re-locked after the ID change

Technical deep dive

Rig

  • Two six-joint SO-101 arms on Feetech STS3215 serial-bus servos, each on its own USB motor bus: a leader with a handle for teleoperation and a follower with a parallel-jaw gripper.
  • Paired RGB cameras, including a top-down view, recorded alongside joint state; calibration and teleoperation run through Hugging Face LeRobot.

Data

  • 50 teleoperated demonstrations in two LeRobot datasets (~49,633 and ~59,998 frames) of calibrated joint commands with synchronized camera frames.
  • Integrity validation before training and camera-dropout handling so a lost stream does not silently corrupt an episode.

Policies

  • ACT (Action Chunking Transformer) trained from scratch on a ResNet-18 vision backbone, 30,000- and 60,000-step runs.
  • SmolVLA vision-language-action model fine-tuned for 20,000 steps on the same demos.
  • Checkpoints selected from physical rollouts, not from training loss.

Evaluation

  • Resumable 50-trial rollout scripts; placement error measured from rollout video; a failure taxonomy over the missed trials.
  • ACT: 46/50 successes at a fixed cube position, 2.49 cm mean placement error among successes. Local inference ~18 ms (ACT) vs ~251 ms (SmolVLA).
  • Caveat: demo start positions varied under 1 cm, so this is not a generalization result; randomized-position evaluation is unfinished.

The Lock=1 bug

Follower IDs reverted after power cycles, and wrist-roll and gripper ended up sharing one Feetech ID, which blocked calibration and with it the whole dataset pipeline. Reproduced it across a power cycle, followed the ID write path in the motor-bus setup, and found the EEPROM lock was never re-set after the write. Patched the setup to write Lock=1 after ID or baud changes, verified IDs across reboots, then recalibrated.

Team project with a second SJSU research assistant. Owned arm bring-up and calibration, teleoperation data collection, the rollout and evaluation scripts, camera-dropout handling and the motor-bus patch. The 46/50 and latency figures are reported in the collaborator-maintained fork (aakashvardhan/so101-lerobot).
