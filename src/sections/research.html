SJSU · Graduate Research Assistant

Human-centered AI research

Applied Data Science × Human Factors × Audiology. Two studies: objective measures of visual attention from eye tracking, and AI-detected obstacles turned into wheelchair warnings people can act on. In the 7-participant eye-tracking pilot, fixation count was the strongest task discriminator; in the published 24-participant wheelchair study, seatback vibration plus instructional cues produced faster reactions.

What the study measured

  • Seven participants, ages 19–36, across three attention tasks: global image description, selective side-by-side difference spotting, and sustained sequential comparison
  • Pupil Labs Core signals: gaze, fixation count and percentage, blink count and percentage, pupil diameter, task duration
  • Per-trial timestamps, invalid-sample handling, heatmaps and statistical comparison across conditions
  • Fixation count — the strongest task discriminator in the small pilot

The broader Body Brain Connect track adds fNIRS hemodynamic signals; that joint analysis is not part of the published eye-tracking paper. Proposed applications in accessible UI, rehabilitation and adaptive HMI are motivating directions, not yet field-tested.

Published apparatus & findings

  • Onboard GPU laptop; ZED 2i RGB + depth at 30 fps; pretrained YOLOv10n pedestrian detection (confidence 0.90); box-center depth drives the ~1 m trigger
  • Measured ~1.65 s haptic actuation lag relative to audio, compensated by movement speed × actuation delay
  • 24 able-bodied participants × 24 trials, 12 warning designs (vibration location × cue type × information type)
  • Seatback vibration + instructional cues → faster reactions and higher reported usefulness than audio-only or informative cues; accuracy did not differ across conditions

Ayush's contributions: perception-to-HMI integration, warning timing and cue logic, timestamped response-time and usefulness capture, and comparative analysis (the Elsevier article credits Software, Methodology and Investigation). The published apparatus is separate from the Jetson/LiDAR C++17 prototype in the Robots chapter. The study evaluates response timing and usefulness, not demonstrated real-world crash reduction.
